# Hooks and Introductions

The opening must win attention, identify the subject quickly, meet a real
reader concern, and promise a payoff the body delivers.

Useful short-form openings include a grounded surprise, sharp contrast,
specific result, consequential question, or direct reframing. Put the bottom
line early. Avoid dictionary definitions, generic scene-setting, empty
questions, vague superlatives, and statistics unrelated to the argument.

Test the hook against the body: if removing the body makes the hook misleading,
rewrite it. Never increase certainty or drama beyond the supplied evidence.
