# Short-Form Narrated Video Scripts

Use this method when the selected brief calls for a narrated product concept,
explainer, thought-leadership clip, or case-study video of roughly 30–120
seconds. The approved brief remains authoritative for audience, objective,
claims, CTA, format, and platform.

## Editorial method

Choose one central argument and build a spoken progression around it. For a
problem-to-transformation concept video, prefer:

1. Open on the specific loss, frustration, or tension.
2. Make the manual or existing alternative feel concrete.
3. Explain why the obvious solution is insufficient.
4. Reveal the product or method through an observable transformation.
5. Show the editorial judgment that protects usefulness and meaning.
6. Accumulate the resulting assets or consequences.
7. Close on what continues, changes, or becomes possible.

Do not read a feature list aloud. Let each capability answer the problem
established immediately before it. Keep sentences concise, conversational,
and comfortable to say in one breath. Avoid generic openings, inflated
adjectives, unsupported comparisons, and abstract claims about AI.

## Production-ready output

When the requested artifact supports structured long-form text, return:

- one-sentence central argument;
- suggested title and estimated runtime;
- timestamped sections covering the requested duration;
- a `VISUAL` direction for every section;
- a `NARRATION` label for every spoken section, with every spoken word inside
  a fenced `text` block;
- sparse suggested on-screen text; and
- an alternative hook and alternative closing line.

Estimate timing from natural narration, then revise timestamps so sections are
contiguous and the final time matches the stated runtime. Do not force this
shape into a platform contract that cannot represent it; return the existing
bounded unsupported-format or insufficient-evidence outcome instead.

## Visual direction

Make the transformation visible. Name the exact object, state change, camera
or layout behavior, and relationship to the spoken line. Useful treatments
include transcript passages resolving into a topic map, evidence-linked
highlights becoming selected moments, edit handles closing around disfluencies,
waveforms changing before and after cleanup, and one source branching into
distinct vertical-video, quote, thread, and summary cards.

Do not write directions such as “show AI,” “show social media,” or “show a
person using a laptop.” Do not imply an interface, processing stage, asset, or
external result that the approved production context does not support.

## Meaning and evidence

For source-derived scripts, preserve the speaker's position, qualifications,
causality, and uncertainty. A cleaned sound bite may remove filler, stutters,
redundant starts, or long pauses only when the remaining words preserve the
original meaning and natural cadence. Never splice separate statements into a
new claim, invent connective speech, attribute unidentified speakers, or claim
audio cleanup or rendering has occurred without a verified artifact.

Product claims, comparisons, customer outcomes, metrics, quotes, and available
features require supplied evidence IDs. Writing guidance and visual metaphors
are not evidence. If the brief asks for an unsupported capability, omit it or
return the existing bounded insufficient-evidence behavior.
