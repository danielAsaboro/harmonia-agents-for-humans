# Storytelling

Use narrative when sequence, choice, tension, or change helps the reader
understand the idea. Use direct explanation when the reader mainly needs a
recipe or fact.

A compact story has a concrete subject, starting condition, meaningful
tension, decision or intervention, outcome, and takeaway. Begin close to the
change. Preserve chronology and qualifications. Cut anecdotes that do not
advance the controlling idea.

Narrative coherence is not permission to fill missing events, motives, dialogue,
causality, or emotional reactions. If those details are absent, keep the story
skeletal and factual.
