# Campaign and portfolio architecture

## Load when

Ryan must define pillars and themes, allocate content across a horizon, balance
repetition with novelty, or revise an undifferentiated collection of ideas.

## Questions answered

- Which recurring messages should compound across the campaign?
- Which distinct themes give those messages new audience, funnel, or use-case
  framings?
- How much should exploit proven evidence versus explore bounded hypotheses?
- What is feasible at the company's present content maturity and capacity?

## Method

1. Convert the thesis into two to four mutually distinct pillars. Each pillar
   has one strategic purpose, not a broad subject label.
2. Cross-cut pillars by supplied audience, pain, funnel job, source moment, or
   use case to produce distinct campaign themes.
3. Classify opportunities as exploit, extend, or explore. Exploit uses verified
   source/performance evidence; extend applies a proven message to another
   supplied context; explore tests a declared assumption.
4. Prefer a small portfolio that can be executed well. Reuse core messages with
   new evidence or framing rather than generating novelty for its own sake.
5. Give experimental themes lower confidence and explicit success criteria.
6. Remove themes that do not support the thesis, objective, or conversion.

## Required inputs

Thesis, horizon, priority audiences, Nimi evidence, verified performance,
eligible memory, supported formats, and known constraints.

## Expected contract effect

Focused `pillars`, `campaignThemes`, `contentMix`, `priorityRules`, and a brief
portfolio Temi can operationalize. It does not choose calendar dates.

## Failure modes

One pillar per random topic, identical themes with renamed titles, unbounded
experimentation, copying a past winner without current relevance, or optimizing
volume past the quality/capacity boundary.

## Evidence and authority

Performance-led allocation cites verified performance IDs. Memory-led
recommendations cite eligible fact IDs and remain advisory. Exploration is an
assumption, not market evidence or permission to act.
