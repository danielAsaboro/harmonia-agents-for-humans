# Funnel, CTA, and measurement

## Load when

The strategy must connect content activity to an audience transition,
conversion, CTA, KPI, target, or success criterion.

## Questions answered

- What should this audience do or believe next?
- Which CTA is proportionate to the funnel stage?
- Which KPI measures the intended transition rather than convenient activity?
- What result would justify continuing, changing, or stopping the strategy?

## Method

1. Define the audience's current state and the single next state content should
   influence.
2. Match CTA intent to funnel stage: learn or inspect at awareness; compare or
   evaluate at consideration; complete the supplied conversion at conversion;
   deepen use or advocate later in the funnel.
3. Use the operator-supplied intended conversion. Do not invent offers, forms,
   trials, downloads, or product actions.
4. Select a primary KPI tied to the transition and supporting diagnostic metrics
   only when measurement is available.
5. Write success criteria with a timeframe or evaluation interval appropriate
   to the horizon, without fabricating a target baseline.
6. Align every brief's objective, CTA, conversion, and KPI with the strategy.

## Required inputs

Business/campaign objectives, funnel stage, intended conversion, audience,
verified performance, measurement constraints, and horizon.

## Expected contract effect

Consistent `funnelIntent`, conversions, `ctaGuidance`, `kpis`,
`successCriteria`, and brief-level alignment.

## Failure modes

Traffic as a universal KPI, a conversion CTA at awareness without support,
targets invented from no baseline, mismatched brief KPIs, or treating
correlation as attribution.

## Evidence and authority

Objectives and conversions cite operator context. Historical benchmarks cite
verified performance IDs. Proposed targets are assumptions when no baseline is
supplied. A CTA recommendation never executes an effect.
