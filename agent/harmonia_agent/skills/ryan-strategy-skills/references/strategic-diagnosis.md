# Strategic diagnosis

## Load when

Objectives compete, the audience is broad, pains are undifferentiated, or the
content request names deliverables before establishing the business problem.

## Questions answered

- Which business constraint can content credibly influence in this horizon?
- Which supplied audience has the strongest combination of pain, relevance,
  evidence, and conversion fit?
- Is the immediate job awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, or
  advocacy?
- What must change in the audience's knowledge, belief, or action?

## Method

1. Separate business objectives from campaign objectives. Choose one primary
   outcome and treat the rest as constraints or secondary outcomes.
2. Rank supplied audiences by pain severity, product relevance, evidence
   strength, and proximity to the intended conversion.
3. Distinguish the immediate product-related pain from adjacent pains that may
   reach the right audience earlier. Adjacent pains remain assumptions unless
   supplied customer evidence supports them.
4. State the current audience condition, desired change, funnel stage, and
   intended conversion as one causal diagnosis.
5. Reject objectives that content cannot measure or influence within the
   planning horizon.

## Required inputs

Company and campaign context IDs, supplied audience segments and pains, funnel
stage, intended conversion, and relevant Nimi/performance evidence.

## Expected contract effect

Sharper objectives, `audiencePriorities`, `funnelIntent`, conversions, and brief
objectives. It does not add fields or replace the typed schema.

## Failure modes

Choosing every audience, treating reach as the business objective, inventing
buyer research, confusing user pain with buyer pain, or selecting a conversion
the product context does not support.

## Evidence and authority

Audience and pain claims cite operator context or supplied evidence. Adjacent
pain hypotheses are bounded assumptions with lower confidence. Diagnosis never
authorizes approval, policy exceptions, scheduling, or effects.
